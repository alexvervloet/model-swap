# Fleet register

Meridian Ferries operates six vessels across three routes. This register is the
authority on vessel capacity and fitted equipment. Where another document refers
to "the vessel's capacity" or "fitted spaces", it means the figures here.

## Class notes

**Coastal.** The three Coastal vessels are the only ones certified to carry
vehicles, and the only ones that sail the Kilmore route. Brindle Bay predates
the 2009 accessibility standard and was retrofitted, which is why it has four
wheelchair spaces rather than six. All three have a covered vehicle deck and an
enclosed passenger lounge.

**Estuary.** Nine Pines and Tern carry foot passengers and bicycles only. Their
twenty racks per vessel are the largest bicycle capacity in the fleet, which is
deliberate: the Ashcombe crossing is part of the coastal cycle route and summer
demand is almost entirely cyclists.

**Heritage.** Ardmore is a 1968 wooden vessel operated for the Harbour Circular
only. She has no lift, no accessible toilet and no wheelchair spaces, and she
cannot be made compliant without destroying the fabric that makes her worth
keeping. Bookings for the Harbour Circular carry a mandatory accessibility
notice for that reason. She does not sail in winter.

## Out of service

MV Lachlan, a Coastal vessel, was withdrawn in March 2024 after a gearbox
failure and has not been replaced. Until a replacement enters service the
Kilmore route runs on two vessels with the third Coastal vessel as relief cover
across all routes, which is the constraint behind the winter timetable
reductions.

## Refit schedule

Each Coastal vessel is out of service for annual refit for fourteen days between
January and March, one at a time and never overlapping. Estuary vessels refit
for seven days in November. Ardmore is surveyed each spring before returning to
service and does not run a winter refit, because she does not run a winter
service.
