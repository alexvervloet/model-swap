# Incident reporting

## What counts as an incident

Five categories, and anything that fits one is reported whether or not harm
resulted.

**Injury.** Any injury to a passenger or crew member, however minor.
**Near miss.** An event that could have caused injury, damage or pollution and
did not, including a collision avoided by a late manoeuvre.
**Pollution.** Any discharge to the water, including a few litres of fuel or
hydraulic oil.
**Security.** An unattended item not claimed within 15 minutes, a threat, an
unauthorised person in a restricted area, or a passenger refused carriage on
security grounds.
**Damage.** Damage to the vessel, to a berth, or to a passenger's vehicle or
property.

An accessibility failure, meaning a booked wheelchair space or booked assistance
that was not provided, is reported as an incident in its own right and not only
through complaints.

## Timescales

| Category | Reported within |
|---|---|
| Pollution | Immediately, before any other report |
| Injury requiring treatment ashore | 2 hours |
| Injury treated on board | 12 hours |
| Security | 12 hours |
| Near miss | 24 hours |
| Damage | 24 hours |
| Accessibility failure | 24 hours |

The clock runs from the time the incident is known to any crew member, not from
the time the report is written or the end of the shift.

## Who reports and who signs

Any crew member may raise a report and is expected to. The Master reviews and
signs every report from their vessel before the end of the sailing day. The
Safety Manager reviews all reports within 5 working days and decides whether an
investigation is opened.

A report is never returned to be softened. A Master who disagrees with a crew
member's account records the disagreement alongside it rather than editing it,
and both accounts go forward.

## Regulator notification

Anything reportable to the regulator goes within 72 hours of the incident, and
that duty sits with the Safety Manager. Reportable events include any injury
requiring hospital admission, any pollution incident regardless of volume, any
collision or grounding, and any structural failure.

The 72-hour duty is not paused by an internal investigation. A report goes on
the facts known at the time and is supplemented later.

## Review

Reports are reviewed monthly by the Safety Committee, which looks for repeats
rather than for individual fault. Near misses are the category the review cares
most about, on the reasoning that a fleet reporting few near misses is one that
has stopped reporting them rather than one that has stopped having them.
