# Accessibility

## Booking assistance

Assistance can be requested when booking or at any time before travel.
Twenty-four hours notice lets us guarantee it. Less than that, and we provide
what we can on the day, which on the Coastal and Estuary vessels is almost
always everything.

Assistance is never a condition of travel. A passenger who arrives without
having booked assistance is not refused carriage and is not asked to wait for a
later sailing.

## Wheelchair spaces

Wheelchair spaces are fitted per vessel; the fleet register holds the numbers.
Spaces can be reserved with a booking and are held until 20 minutes before
departure, after which unclaimed spaces are released to passengers waiting at
the terminal.

MV Brindle Bay has four spaces rather than the six on the other two Coastal
vessels. It was built in 2004 and retrofitted, and the retrofit could not
achieve six without losing the accessible toilet. Where a booking requires more
than four wheelchair spaces on one sailing, the terminal office checks which
vessel is rostered before confirming.

## MV Ardmore and the Harbour Circular

MV Ardmore is a 1968 wooden vessel with no lift, no accessible toilet and no
wheelchair spaces. She cannot be brought to the current standard without
destroying the structure that makes her worth preserving, and the company has
decided to keep her in service rather than withdraw her.

Every Harbour Circular booking therefore carries a mandatory accessibility
notice at the point of sale, stating plainly what is not available. The Harbour
Circular is the only Meridian service that is not accessible, and there is no
accessible alternative for it, because no other vessel operates that route.

## Assistance dogs

Assistance dogs travel free on every route, including the Harbour Circular. They
need no booking, are not counted against the limit on pets, and are not subject
to the pet policy in any respect. They may accompany their owner anywhere a
passenger may go, including the passenger lounge.

Staff do not ask for documentation for an assistance dog.

## On board

The Coastal vessels have a lift between the vehicle deck and the passenger
lounge, an accessible toilet on the lounge deck, and a hearing loop at the
information desk and in the lounge. The Estuary vessels have step-free access
throughout by design, an accessible toilet, and no hearing loop.

Boarding is by the passenger ramp on all vessels except at Kilmore at low water,
where the ramp gradient exceeds 1:12 for approximately 90 minutes either side of
low water and crew assistance is offered to any passenger who wants it.

## If something goes wrong

An accessibility failure, meaning a booked space or a booked assistance that was
not provided, is logged as an incident and not only as a complaint. See the
incident reporting policy. The passenger is told this has been done.
